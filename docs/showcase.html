00 · showcase

Two instruments · one of them is the library

Two things
a packet does.

A datagram is cut apart and put back together by the numbers in its header. An address is asked a short series of questions until one of them answers. The first happens in a stack, not in this package; the second is this package's whole job. Both are drawn here with the numbers they actually have.

01 · reassembly

RFC 791 · offset in eights · the more-fragments flag

One datagram, six fragments,
no order.

The wire hands over fragment 4 first, then 1, then 6. Where each one belongs is not in the order it arrives but in its offset field: thirteen bits, counted in eight-byte units from the start of the original. Scroll, and the buffer fills around the holes.

id 0x1a2b 47 bytes of payload six fragments scroll to deliver

0 / 47 bytes · 0 / 6 fragments

    fig. 1 The payload is the sentence the exhibit is about — the offset says where; the length says how much — in ASCII, so a gap in the buffer is a gap in the reading. No library call is made here: fragmentation is a datagram operation and this package never holds a datagram. What is exact is the arithmetic — offsets on eight-byte boundaries, the flag clear on the last fragment only, and the byte counts adding to 47.

    02 · trace

    seven questions · the first yes stops it

    An address,
    asked seven times.

    Classification is not one comparison. It is a walk down the rows of two registries — loopback, multicast, link local, private — and the walk ends at the first row that claims the address. A public address climbs all seven rungs before the last one answers; ::1 stops at the second; and the one block that belongs to neither side reaches the bottom with nothing to show for it.

    03 · why these two

    exact, both ways

    A question with a checkable answer.

    A fragment is 47 bytes of ASCII and a set of offsets; an address is 32 or 128 bits. Neither needs a socket, and neither answer depends on the network being there. That is the whole shape of this package: values in, text out, and a refusal that says where it stopped being possible.

    The guide walks the surface in ten steps; the explorer takes blocks apart with the same compiled build; the reference is generated from the interface the compiler emits.
